# Trov — Teixeira's Ground Truth

**Sources:** `MGT470-course material/DD Course (UCSD Winter 2026) Session 4.pdf`; `MGT470-course material/WI26MGT470-999016DigitalDisruptionTeixeira-PT_1089720730181398/MonetizingInsuranceAtTrov.pdf`

## CVC (as Teixeira drew it in Session N)
Session 4 teaches the general decoupling recipe and uses Trov as the case anchor. The case PDF's Exhibit 2, "A Typical Insurance Customer's Value Chain," draws the insurance CVC as:

1. Evaluate options.
2. Choose one insurer.
3. Request insurance policy.
4. Sign and pay premium.
5. Make claim, get paid.
6. Cancel insurance.

The exhibit notes that not all customers made claims or received payments. The case narrative also emphasizes the on/off control of insurance coverage through Trov's mobile app.

## Weak link Teixeira pointed at
The weak link is the value-charging and effort-heavy insurance-policy stage: signing up for coverage, paying premiums continuously, and managing coverage that may not match when the customer actually needs protection. The broader insurance pain points named in the case are bureaucracy, claim delays, lack of flexibility, and policy prices.

## Decoupling pattern Teixeira describes
Trov is primarily value-charging decoupling: it decouples the customer's need for protection from continuous insurance payment by letting users activate coverage only for an item and duration they choose. It also reduces value-eroding friction in requesting and managing coverage through a mobile app. The case's analytical punchline is that decoupling can create customer value without automatically creating a profitable business model.

## Teixeira's strategic takeaway / what he says incumbents and disruptors should do
- Decoupling is a five-step process: map the CVC, identify value type, find the weak link, increase specialization forces by reducing monetary/time/effort cost, and anticipate competitive response.
- Trov demonstrates that customers may love a decoupled experience while the direct-to-consumer economics remain unattractive.
- The strategic decision is what to do with single-item coverage: keep investing as a learning/narrative bridge, withdraw support, or gradually shift toward B2B/partnership products such as ridesharing insurance, renters/homeowners, or retailer warranty-like insurance.
- The case highlights a gap between user growth/retention and profitability because CAC rose faster than contribution margin.

## Open questions Teixeira flags in lecture
- What should Trov do with single-item coverage?
- Can Trov lower CAC through scalable distribution partnerships?
- Should single-item coverage remain a consumer-facing product, become a bridge to B2B products, or be abandoned?
- Does decoupling alone produce a sustainable business model, or only a customer-value wedge?
