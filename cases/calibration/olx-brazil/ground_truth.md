# OLX Brazil — Teixeira's Ground Truth

**Sources:** `MGT470-course material/DD Course (UCSD Winter 2026) Session 9.pdf`; `MGT470-course material/WI26MGT470-999016DigitalDisruptionTeixeira-PT_1089720730181398/WhereToGrowNextAtOnlineMarketplaceOLX.pdf`

## CVC (as Teixeira drew it in Session N)
Session 9 does not provide a fully labeled OLX-specific CVC in extractable slide text. It teaches "Growth by Coupling" after an initial decoupling beachhead and uses Alibaba's chain as a visible example: choose device/OS, search online, shop, talk to seller, pay, receive item.

For OLX Brazil, the case text supports a marketplace CVC:

1. Sellers post/list items.
2. Buyers browse/search classified ads.
3. Buyer and seller communicate or match.
4. Buyer and seller meet or arrange the transaction offline.
5. Payment and exchange occur outside OLX.
6. Related needs such as financing, insurance, escrow, payments, or shipping may arise around the transaction.

The key point is that OLX historically owned matchmaking/posting, not the full transaction.

## Weak link Teixeira pointed at
For the original OLX model, the weak link was the inefficient local classified-ad matching process: OLX made it easier to list, discover, and match buyers and sellers. In Session 9, however, the central issue is no longer the initial decoupling but where to grow after the beachhead. The weak link for the next move is the uncaptured adjacent transaction layer: financing, trust, escrow, payments, insurance, and other services around cars, real estate, and used-goods exchanges.

## Decoupling pattern Teixeira describes
OLX began as a decoupler of classifieds matchmaking from traditional offline classifieds. Session 9 frames the next strategic move as coupling, not further decoupling: after stealing an activity, a company can add adjacent links in the current customer chain or add new chains. For OLX, the live strategic choice is among focusing on core classifieds, adding new verticals, or coupling adjacent transaction services to the existing marketplace.

## Teixeira's strategic takeaway / what he says incumbents and disruptors should do
- Companies in growth phases often take too many routes at once; lack of focus is a concern.
- Growth should leverage the firm's strengths and avoid moves that are easy for stronger competitors to copy.
- Do not leave the core business under-protected while expanding outward.
- Coupling can reduce CAC and create customer-side synergies, but it may require entering very different businesses and building new skills.
- For OLX specifically, the case frames three routes: focus on core cars/real estate, launch new verticals such as jobs/agriculture/auto parts, or add adjacent services such as financing, payments, insurance, and escrow.

## Open questions Teixeira flags in lecture
- Which growth strategy should OLX pursue in the coming years?
- Should OLX stick with core verticals, expand into new verticals, or enter adjacent services?
- How can OLX double buyers and sellers, reach a $4 billion valuation target by 2023, and defend against Facebook, Mercado Libre, niche vertical platforms, and Big Tech copycats?
- Which opportunities create customer-side synergies without spreading resources too thin?
